\section{Introduction and main results}
\label{sec:introduction}



\subsection{The SYK model}\label{subsec:SYK-model}
The Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev (SYK) model is a quantum-mechanical model of \(N\) fermions with random interactions between all pairs of particles. It originates in the work of Sachdev and Ye on random quantum magnets \cite{SachdevYe1993} and in Kitaev’s subsequent formulation in terms of Majorana fermions \cite{Kitaev2015}. Later work by Maldacena, Stanford, and others clarified its large-\(N\) behavior and revealed deep connections to quantum chaos and two-dimensional quantum gravity \cite{MaldacenaStanford2016}. These features make the SYK model a useful setting for studying strongly interacting quantum many-body systems, quantum chaos, and quantum gravity.

Depending on how the inverse temperature \(\beta=\beta_N\) scales with \(N\), the model exhibits several distinct regimes. For fixed \(\beta\), one obtains the standard finite-temperature large-\(N\) regime; when \(1\ll \beta\ll N\), the system enters the conformal regime, characterized by strong interactions and non-Fermi liquid behavior. At lower temperatures, \(N\ll \beta\), the quantum-Schwarzian regime emerges, in which finite-\(N\) effects and quantum fluctuations of the Schwarzian mode play a central role. Finally, when \(\beta\) is exponentially large in \(N\), the partition function is mainly determined by the eigenvalues near the edge of the spectrum. In this regime, random matrix theory (RMT) describes their fluctuations. Such a range of crossover phenomena is rare in a strongly interacting model that remains analytically tractable in suitable limits.

Previous mathematical work has extensively studied the limiting free energy of the SYK model for fixed $\beta$ \cite{GPSZ2026,He2026}. Moreover, Anschuetz, Gamarnik, and Kiani \cite{AGK2025} established a universality result showing that the limiting free energy is the same for a broad class of interaction distributions.  This naturally raises the question of how physical quantities depend on the interactions. Motivated by this question, we study fluctuations of the free energy and show that, for sufficiently small \(\beta\), the suitably rescaled free energy converges to a Gaussian distribution.

Fix an even integer $q\geq4$, and let $N$ be even.  Write
\[
  \calI_N:=\{I\subset[N]:|I|=q\},\qquad
  \sigma_N^2:=\frac{(q-1)!}{N^{q-1}},
\]
and let $G:= (g_I)_{I\in\calI_N}$ be independent standard Gaussian random
variables.  Let $\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_N$ be Majorana matrices satisfying
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq: clifford relation}
  \{\psi_i,\psi_j\}=2\delta_{ij}\I
\end{equation}
For
$I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_q\}$ set
\[
  \psi_I:=\mathrm i^{q/2}\psi_{i_1}\cdots\psi_{i_q}.
\]
Then $\psi_I$ is Hermitian, $\psi_I^2=\I$, and $\norm{\psi_I}=1$.  Define
\[
  H_N(G):=\sigma_N\sum_{I\in\calI_N}g_I\psi_I,
  \qquad
  Z_N(z;G):=\tr e^{-zH_N(G)}.
\]
Here $\tr:=2^{-N/2}\operatorname{Tr}$ denotes the normalized trace; in
particular, $Z_N(0;G)=1$.  This normalization does not affect the
relative fluctuations studied below. We sometime write $Z_N(\beta) := Z_N(\beta;G)$ for brevity. 
For real $\beta$, set
\[
 Y_N(\beta):=  Y_N(\beta;G):=\frac{Z_N(\beta;G)}{\mathbb E Z_N(\beta)}.
\]
Thus $\E Y_N(\beta)=1$.
Throughout, all limits are taken as $N\to\infty$ through even integers.

\subsection{Main results}
It is studied the limiting behavior of the logarithmic partition function in high-temperature regime:
\begin{theorem}\label{thm: input}
There exists $\tilde{\beta}_q>0$ such that for any $\beta\in [-\tilde{\beta}_q,\tilde{\beta}_q]$, there exists  $\ell_q(\beta)$ such that
\begin{align*}
    \lim_{N\to \infty} N^{-1} \log Z_N=     \lim_{N\to \infty} N^{-1} \log \mathbb E[Z_N] =\ell_p(\beta)\qquad \text{ a.s.}
\end{align*}
Moreover, $\ell_p(\beta)$ is differentiable over $(-\tilde{\beta}_q,\tilde{\beta}_q)$. 
\end{theorem}
Our main result is the following fluctuation resutls:
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:partition-clt}
{
}
There exists $\beta_q\in (0,\tilde{\beta}_q]$ such that for any $\beta\in (-\beta_q,\beta_q)$,
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-relative-clt}
  N^{q/2-1}\left(Y_N(\beta)-1\right)
  \Longrightarrow
  \,\N\left(0,\frac{q!}{2}\beta^2(\ell_q'(\beta))^2\right).
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:standardized-clt}
  \frac{Z_N(\beta)-\E Z_N(\beta)}
  {\sqrt{\Var Z_N(\beta)}}
  \Longrightarrow\N(0,1).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:free-energy-clt}
With the same $\beta_p$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:partition-clt}, for any $\beta\in (-\beta_q,\beta_q)$,
\[
  N^{q/2-1}\left(
    \log Z_N(\beta)-\E\log Z_N(\beta)
  \right)
  \Longrightarrow
  \,\N\left(0,\frac{q!}{2}\,\beta^2(\ell_q'(\beta))^2\right).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{remark}
The high-temperature threshold $\beta_q$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:partition-clt} need not coincide with the threshold
$\tilde{\beta}_q$ in Theorem~\ref{thm: input}.
  In fact, our proof needs small $|\beta|$ to control
the combinatorial terms arising in higher chaos. 
  We do not pursue such an extension here, nor do we
conjecture whether the same fluctuation results hold at all
temperatures.
\end{remark}
\subsection{Previous work}

Rigorous results on the high-temperature annealed and quenched free-energy limits were recently obtained in \cite{GPSZ2026}. More recently, He \cite{He2026} identified the fixed-temperature free-energy limit of the Gaussian SYK model with the Schwinger--Dyson pressure and used this result to determine the spectral edge. These works focus on the leading-order behavior of the free energy, while the purpose of this paper is to study its leading fluctuations in the high-temperature regime.

Feng, Tian, and Wei \cite{FTW2018} proved a central limit theorem for linear eigenvalue statistics of the Gaussian SYK Hamiltonian. Their result applies to a fixed test function \(f\), under suitable regularity assumptions on \(f\) and \(f'\). In their normalization, however, the partition function corresponds to
\[
f_{N,\beta}(x):=e^{-\beta\tau_N x},
\qquad \tau_N\asymp\sqrt{N},
\]
so both the test function and its derivative depend on \(N\). Therefore, their fixed-test-function theorem does not apply directly to \(Z_N(\beta)\).


\subsection{Outline of the proof}

Recall that $Y_n(\beta):= {Z_N(\beta)}/{\mathbb E Z_N(\beta)}$.  
By applying the chaos expansion, since Clifford sign-gauge symmetry eliminates the first Gaussian chaos and
identifies the second chaos exactly, we have the following:
\[
  Y_N(\beta)-1=
  A_N(\beta)Q_N+\mathcal R_N(\beta),
  \]
  where
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-N-FTW-coefficient}
        A_N(\beta):=\frac{\beta}{2}\partial_\beta\log \mathbb E Z_N(\beta),
    \qquad
      Q_N:=\frac1{|\mathcal I_N|}\sum_I(g_I^2-1).
    \end{equation}
Here $\mathcal R_N$ is the sum of the Gaussian chaoses of $Y_N$ of orders at least
three.  Thus the leading  term is a normalized sum of $\mathcal I_N$
independent centered variables.  The main task is to prove
\[
  \norm{\mathcal R_N(\beta)}_2^2=o(N^{-(q-2)}).
\]

{
Indeed, a Duhamel derivative bound and the sign-gauge
parity rule give
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_{r\geq3}\lVert P_r Y_N(\beta)\rVert_2^2
  \leq
  \sum_{r\geq3}(\beta\sigma_N)^{2r}
  \sum_{\substack{|\alpha|=r\\ \alpha\ \mathrm{even}}}
  \frac{1}{\alpha!}.
\end{equation*}
Using a combinatorial argument, we have that the higher-order chaoses are 
bounded by  $O(N^{-3(q/2-1)})=o(N^{-(q-2)})$.
}

Moreover, the annealed free-energy input and convexity imply
\[
  \frac{A_N(\beta)}N
  \longrightarrow \frac{\beta}{2}\ell_q'(\beta).
\]
Since $M_N\sim N^q/q!$, the classical central limit theorem for $Q_N$   gives the first claim. 

To obtain the second claim, the logarithmic fluctuation, observe that
\begin{align*}
    &N^{q/2-1}\bigl(\log Z_N(\beta)-\E\log Z_N(\beta)\bigr)\\
  &=N^{q/2-1}\bigl(\log Y_N(\beta)-(Y_N(\beta)-1)\bigr)
   +N^{q/2-1}(Y_N(\beta)-1)-N^{q/2-1}\E\log Y_N(\beta).
\end{align*}
The Taylor expansion $\log(1+x)-x=O(x^2)$, together with the
first claim, shows that the first term  on the right-hand side converges to zero in probability. 
The chaos decomposition gives $\E(Y_N(\beta)-1)^2=O(N^{2-q})$ and
$\mathbb P(Y_N(\beta)<1/2)=O(N^{-3(q/2-1)})$. Together with
$Z_N(\beta)\geq1$, these estimates give
\[
  \E\log Y_N(\beta)=O\!\left(N^{2-q}\right)
  =o\!\left(N^{1-q/2}\right).
\]
Therefore, we conclude the second claim.
