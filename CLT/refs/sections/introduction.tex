\subsection{The SYK model}\label{subsec:SYK-model}
The Sachdev--Ye--Kitaev (SYK) model is a quantum-mechanical model of \(N\) Majorana fermions with random many-body interactions. It originates in the work of Sachdev and Ye on random quantum magnets \cite{SachdevYe1993} and in Kitaev’s subsequent formulation in terms of Majorana fermions \cite{Kitaev2015}. Later work by Maldacena, Stanford, and others clarified its large-\(N\) behavior and revealed deep connections to quantum chaos and two-dimensional quantum gravity \cite{MaldacenaStanford2016}. These features make the SYK model a useful setting for studying strongly interacting quantum many-body systems, quantum chaos, and quantum gravity.

Depending on how the inverse temperature \(\beta=\beta_N\) scales with \(N\), the model exhibits several distinct regimes. For fixed \(\beta\), one obtains the standard finite-temperature large-\(N\) regime; when \(1\ll \beta\ll N\), the system enters the conformal regime, characterized by strong interactions and non-Fermi liquid behavior. At lower temperatures, \(N\ll \beta\), the quantum-Schwarzian regime emerges, in which finite-\(N\) effects and quantum fluctuations of the Schwarzian mode play a central role. Finally, when \(\beta\) is exponentially large in \(N\), the partition function is mainly determined by the eigenvalues near the edge of the spectrum. In this regime, random matrix theory  describes their fluctuations. The appearance of such diverse crossover phenomena is rare in a strongly interacting model that remains analytically tractable in suitable limits.

Previous mathematical work has studied the limiting free energy of the SYK model for fixed $\beta$ \cite{GPSZ2026,He2026}. Moreover, Anschuetz, Gamarnik, and Kiani \cite{AGK2025} established a universality result showing that the limiting free energy is the same for a broad class of interaction distributions.  This naturally raises the question of how physical quantities depend on the interactions. Motivated by this question, we study fluctuations of the free energy and show that, for sufficiently small \(|\beta|\), the suitably rescaled free energy converges to a Gaussian distribution.

Fix an even integer $q\geq4$, and let $N$ be even. Set $[N] := \{1,2,\ldots,N\}$ and write
\[
  \calI_N:=\{I\subset[N]:|I|=q\},\qquad
  \sigma_N^2:=\frac{(q-1)!}{N^{q-1}},
\]
where $|I|$ denotes the cardinality of the set $I$.
Let $G:= (g_I)_{I\in\calI_N}$ be a family of independent standard Gaussian random
variables. We denote the corresponding probability measure and expectation by $\mathbb P$ and $\E$, respectively. Following \cite[Section~2]{GPSZ2026}, we let $\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_N$ be Majorana matrices \textcolor{red}{of size $2^{N/2} \times 2^{N/2}$, which satisfy the following anticommutation relation\footnote{Use a consistent word ``anticommutation relation''}:} \textcolor{blue}{satisfying}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq: clifford relation}
  \{\psi_i,\psi_j\} \textcolor{red}{:= \psi_i \psi_j + \psi_j \psi_i} =2\delta_{ij}\I \textcolor{red}{,}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{red}{where $\I$ denotes the identity matrix.
(NB. The Majorana matrices considered here are $\sqrt{2}$ times those used in \cite{GPSZ2026,He2026}; this is merely a difference in normalization.)}
For
$I = \{i_1, \ldots, i_q\} \in \calI_N$ with $i_1 < \cdots < i_q$, set
\[
  \psi_I\coloneqq\mathrm i^{q/2}\psi_{i_1}\cdots\psi_{i_q}\textcolor{blue}{.}\textcolor{red}{,}
\]
\textcolor{red}{where $\mathrm i$ denotes the imaginary unit. Since Majorana matrices are Hermitian, it follows from \eqref{eq: clifford relation} that }
\textcolor{blue}{Then} $\psi_I$ is \textcolor{red}{also} Hermitian\textcolor{red}{.}\textcolor{blue}{,} \textcolor{red}{Moreover,} $\psi_I^2=\I$\textcolor{blue}{,} and\textcolor{red}{, in particular,} $\norm{\psi_I}=1$\textcolor{blue}{.}\textcolor{red}{, where $\|\cdot\|$ denotes the operator norm of a matrix.}  \textcolor{blue}{Define
\[
  H_N(G):=\sigma_N\sum_{I\in\calI_N}g_I\psi_I,
  \qquad
  Z_N(z;G):=\tr e^{-zH_N(G)}.
\]}
\textcolor{red}{The Hamiltonian of the SYK model is defined by 
\[
  H_N(G):=\sigma_N\sum_{I\in\calI_N}g_I\psi_I.
\]
The corresponding partition function is then given by\footnote{\textcolor{red}{The variable $z$ should be complex? If only real $z$ is enough, we should replace it by $\beta$.}}
\[
  Z_N(\beta) := Z_N(\beta;G):=\tr e^{-\beta H_N(G)},\qquad \beta \in \mathbb R.
\]}
Here\textcolor{red}{,} $\tr:=2^{-N/2}\operatorname{Tr}$ denotes the normalized trace; in
particular, $Z_N(0;G)=1$.  \textcolor{blue}{This normalization does not affect the
relative fluctuations studied below. We sometime write $Z_N(\beta) := Z_N(\beta;G)$ for brevity.} 
For real $\beta$, \textcolor{blue}{set} \textcolor{red}{define the normalized partition function by}
\[
 Y_N(\beta):=  Y_N(\beta;G):=\frac{Z_N(\beta;G)}{\mathbb E Z_N(\beta)}.
\]
Thus $\E Y_N(\beta)=1$.
\textcolor{blue}{Throughout, all limits are taken as $N\to\infty$ through even integers.}

\subsection{Main results}
Here, we state our main results: Theorem~\ref{thm:partition-clt} and Corollary~\ref{cor:free-energy-clt}.
We first recall the convergence of the annealed free energy, which was established for sufficiently small $|\beta|$ in \cite{GPSZ2026} and recently extended to all $\beta$ in \cite{He2026}. Moreover, in \cite{He2026}, the limit is identified with the Schwinger--Dyson pressure. Throughout this article, all limits as $N \to \infty$ are taken through even integers.
\begin{theorem}[{\cite[Theorem~1.1 and Proposition~3.3]{He2026}}]\label{thm: input}
For any $\beta\in \mathbb R$, there exists  $\ell_q(\beta)$ such that
\begin{align*}
    \lim_{N\to \infty} N^{-1} \log \E Z_N =\ell_q(\beta).
\end{align*}
Moreover, $\ell_q(\beta)$ is differentiable in $\beta \in \mathbb R$.
\end{theorem}

Our first main result concerns the fluctuations of the normalized partition function $Y_N(\beta)$.\footnote{\textcolor{red}{At this stage, it might be readable to use $Z_N/\E Z_N$ directly, instead of $Y_N$.}} For $a > 0$, we write $\N(0, a)$ for the centered Gaussian distribution with variance $a$.
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:partition-clt}
There exists $\beta_q\in (0,\infty)$ such that for any $\beta\in (-\beta_q,\beta_q) \setminus \{0\}$,
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-relative-clt}
  N^{q/2-1}\left(Y_N(\beta)-1\right)
  \Longrightarrow
  \,\N\left(0,\frac{q!}{2}\beta^2(\ell_q'(\beta))^2\right)\textcolor{blue}{.}\textcolor{red}{,}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{red}{where the arrow $\Longrightarrow$ means convergence in distribution\footnote{Use $Z_N$.}.}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:standardized-clt}
  \frac{Z_N(\beta)-\E Z_N(\beta)}
  {\sqrt{\Var Z_N(\beta)}}
  \Longrightarrow\N(0,1)\textcolor{blue}{.}\textcolor{red}{,}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{red}{where $\Var Z_N(\beta)$ denotes the variance of $Z_N(\beta)$.}
\end{theorem}

\textcolor{red}{Combining the above theorem with the asymptotic relation $\log x \sim x-1$ as $x \to 1$, we obtain the following result on the fluctuations of the free energy.\footnote{\textcolor{red}{This has been upgraded to a theorem.}}}
\begin{theorem}\label{cor:free-energy-clt}
With the same \textcolor{blue}{$\beta_p$} \textcolor{red}{$\beta_q$} as in Theorem~\ref{thm:partition-clt}, for any $\beta\in (-\beta_q,\beta_q) \setminus \{0\}$,
\[
  N^{q/2-1}\left(
    \log Z_N(\beta)-\E\log Z_N(\beta)
  \right)
  \Longrightarrow
  \,\N\left(0,\frac{q!}{2}\,\beta^2(\ell_q'(\beta))^2\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
\textcolor{blue}{Our proof needs small $|\beta|$ to control the combinatorial terms arising in higher chaos.}
\textcolor{red}{The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:partition-clt} is based on the polynomial chaos expansion of $Y_N(\beta)$, and the smallness of $|\beta|$ is used to control the higher-order chaos terms; see Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-chaos-closure} below.}
We do not pursue an extension  to the whole $\beta\in \mathbb R$ here, nor do we conjecture whether the same fluctuation results hold at all temperatures.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
 As a related work, Feng, Tian, and Wei \cite{FTW2018} proved a central limit theorem for linear eigenvalue statistics of the Hamiltonian $H_N$. Their result applies to a fixed test function \(f\), under suitable regularity assumptions on \(f\) and \(f'\). In their normalization, however, the partition function corresponds to

\]
