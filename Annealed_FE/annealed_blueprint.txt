LEAN FORMALIZATION BLUEPRINT FOR THE ANNEALED SYK FREE-ENERGY FORMULA
====================================================================

Source: `refs/SYK-HT-arxiv.tex`.
Intended final Lean entry point: `SYK/Annealed_FE/main_result.lean`.
Status: detailed implementation blueprint, not a completed proof.

The primary goal is to formalize the annealed statement in Theorem
`theorem:main-result` (TeX lines 562--569).  The formalization should prove the
finite-volume identity from the Rademacher SYK model, reduce it to a coefficient
problem for an exponential generating function, prove the coefficient
asymptotics by the saddle-point argument, and finally identify the tree series
with the Chord-Cavity-Generator expression `mathcal F` used in the paper.

This blueprint deliberately separates the genuine annealed theorem from the
quenched claims discussed at TeX lines 405--421.  The equality/concentration of
quenched and annealed free energies is cited there from outside this TeX file
(the label `eq:quenched-is-annealed` is not defined in the supplied source).
It is not an input to, or a conclusion of, the theorem planned below.


0. EXACT MATHEMATICAL TARGET AND REQUIRED REPAIRS TO THE PAPER STATEMENT
------------------------------------------------------------------------

0.1 Paper conventions and formula

For positive even `q`, even matrix size parameter `n`, and Rademacher couplings,
the paper uses

  sigmaSq(n,q) = (q-1)! / n^(q-1),
  H_SYK = sum_I sigma(n,q) * epsilon_I * psi_I,
  Z_(n,q)(beta) = tr(exp(beta H_SYK)),
  f_(n,q)(beta) = beta^(-1) log(E[Z_(n,q)(beta)]),

where `tr = 2^(-n/2) Tr`, `{psi_i,psi_j}=delta_ij`, and hence
`psi_i^2=(1/2)I` and `psi_I^2=2^(-q)I`; see TeX lines 342 and 374--416.

The auxiliary quantities are

  rho(q,beta) = (((q-1)! * beta^2) / 2^(q+1))^(1/(q-1)),
  mu_v(q,beta) = rho^(v-1) / v! * sum_{T in T_v^sim} b_T,
  Phi(q,beta,z) = sum_{v>=1} mu_v z^v,

and `tStar=tStar(q,beta)` is the unique real root in `(1/2,3/2)` of

  tStar * Phi'(tStar) = 1.

The Chord-Cavity-Generator `H`, the functions `R` and `E`, and the closed
formula are defined at TeX lines 424--559:

  mathcalF(q,beta,z)
    = rho^(-1) * (R(rho*z) - (q-1)*E(rho*z)).

The requested conclusion is

  lim_(n -> infinity, n even) f_(n,q)(beta)/n
    = (mathcalF(tStar) - 1 - log(tStar))/beta.

0.2 Prove pressure first; divide by beta only in a corollary

Theorem `theorem:main-result` says `|beta| <= betaStar`, but both its definition
of `f_(n,q)` and its displayed right-hand side divide by `beta`.  At `beta=0`
those expressions are undefined.  Moreover `mathcalF` as displayed divides by
`rho`, and `rho(q,0)=0`.

The canonical Lean theorem must therefore be the stronger and well-typed
annealed pressure statement

  pressure_(n,q)(beta) := log(E[Z_(n,q)(beta)]) / n,

  pressure_(n,q)(beta) --> Phi(tStar) - 1 - log(tStar).

This theorem is meaningful at `beta=0`; there `Phi(z)=z`, `tStar=1`, and the
limit is zero.  Then prove the exact paper formula as a corollary under
`beta != 0`, using `Phi = mathcalF`.  Optionally define a continuously extended
free-energy density to be zero at `beta=0`, but do not silently use Lean's
field convention `0⁻¹=0` as if it were the paper's definition.

0.3 Make the even-size limit explicit

The Hilbert space dimension `2^(n/2)` and the displayed Majorana construction
require even `n`.  The paper suppresses this in its limit notation.  For fixed
even `q`, use the cofinal enumeration

  def admissibleSize (q k : Nat) : Nat := q + 2*k.

It is even, at least `q`, and enumerates all even `n >= q`.  State every final
`Tendsto` along `k -> admissibleSize q k`.  A later convenience lemma may
translate this to a filter on the subtype `{n // Even n and q <= n}`.

0.4 Correct the pointed-tree identity used to identify `Phi` and `mathcalF`

TeX lines 1348--1360 contain an indexing/double-counting typo.  Since
`hat R(z)=sum_{v>=1} z^v/v! sum_T |V(T)| b_T` already contains the singleton
term `z`, the consistent identity is

  R(z) - (q-1) E(z)
    = sum_{v>=1} z^v/v! * sum_{T in T_v^sim} b_T,

with no additional `+ z` outside that sum.  Otherwise the coefficient of `z`
would be two, contradicting `mu_1=1`, `Phi(z)=z+O(z^2)`, and the definition of
`R`.  The Lean proof must use the corrected identity and include regression
lemmas asserting `coeff 1 = 1` on both sides.

0.5 Recommended final public statements

Use separate names for complex analytic functions and their real restrictions:

  phiC q beta : Complex -> Complex
  phiR q beta t := (phiC q beta (t : Complex)).re
  closedFormulaC q beta : Complex -> Complex
  closedFormulaR q beta t := (closedFormulaC q beta (t : Complex)).re.

Prove these complex functions take real values on real inputs.  Every pressure
or free-energy `Tendsto` below is a theorem in `Real` and therefore uses the
suffix-R functions; contour and analyticity lemmas use the suffix-C functions.

The final file should expose statements morally equivalent to the following.
The exact namespace and implicit arguments can be adjusted once the model
adapter has compiled.

  theorem betaStar_pos
      (q : Nat) (hq2 : 2 <= q) (hqeven : Even q) : 0 < betaStar q

  theorem annealed_pressure_limit
      (q : Nat) (hq2 : 2 <= q) (hqeven : Even q)
      (beta : Real) (hsmall : |beta| <= betaStar q) :
      Tendsto
        (fun k => annealedPressure (admissibleSize q k) q beta)
        atTop
        (nhds (phiR q beta (tStar q beta) - 1 - Real.log (tStar q beta)))

  theorem phiC_eq_closedFormulaC
      (q : Nat) (hq2 : 2 <= q) (hqeven : Even q)
      (beta : Real) (z : Complex) (hsmall : |beta| <= betaStar q)
      (hz : ‖z‖ <= 2) :
      phiC q beta z = closedFormulaC q beta z

  theorem closedFormulaC_eq_paperClosedFormulaC
      (q : Nat) (beta : Real) (z : Complex) (hbeta : beta != 0)
      (hz : ‖z‖ <= 2) :
      closedFormulaC q beta z = paperClosedFormulaC q beta z

  theorem annealed_freeEnergy_density_limit
      (q : Nat) (hq2 : 2 <= q) (hqeven : Even q)
      (beta : Real) (hbeta : beta != 0)
      (hsmall : |beta| <= betaStar q) :
      Tendsto
        (fun k => annealedFreeEnergy (admissibleSize q k) q beta /
          (admissibleSize q k : Real))
        atTop
        (nhds ((closedFormulaR q beta (tStar q beta) - 1 -
          Real.log (tStar q beta)) / beta))

Also expose `tStar_mem_Ioo : tStar q beta ∈ Set.Ioo (1/2) (3/2)` and the complex
identity `tStar_stationary : (tStar q beta : Complex) *
deriv (phiC q beta) (tStar q beta : Complex) = 1`, under the same
high-temperature hypothesis.  Do not hide the
choice of the stationary point inside the final theorem.

Finally add a one-line existential wrapper taking `betaStar q` as witness, so
the public statement also mirrors the quantifier order of TeX Theorem
`theorem:main-result`.

1.3 Suggested paper-model API

Create `SYK/Annealed_FE/PaperModel.lean`.  Suggested definitions (pseudocode;
casts and typeclass parameters will need routine adjustment) are:

  structure HTParams where
    q : Nat
    q_even : Even q
    q_ge_two : 2 <= q

All subsequent definitions and theorems should take `P : HTParams` (or carry
`hqeven` and `hq2` explicitly).  For readability, later pseudocode writes only
`q`; it never asserts the rho, excess, or Clifford lemmas for `q=0` or `q=1`.

  abbrev Edge (N q : Nat) := InteractionIndex N q

  def sigmaSq (N q : Nat) : Real :=
    (Nat.factorial (q-1) : Real) / (N : Real)^(q-1)

  noncomputable def sigma (N q : Nat) : Real := Real.sqrt (sigmaSq N q)

  noncomputable def paperInteractionScale (q : Nat) : Real :=
    (Real.sqrt 2)⁻¹ ^ q

  def SpinConfig (N q : Nat) := Edge N q -> Fin 2

  def rademacher (omega : SpinConfig N q) (I : Edge N q) : Real :=
    if omega I = 0 then 1 else -1

  noncomputable def paperInteraction
      (M : MajoranaSystem N (Matrix d d Complex)) (I : Edge N q) :=
    (paperInteractionScale q : Complex) •
      ((phase q) • M.psiProduct I)

In actual Lean it is usually easier to avoid a negative real power here and
write `(Real.sqrt 2)⁻¹ ^ q`, or to multiply the whole existing Hamiltonian by
`(Real.sqrt 2)⁻¹ ^ q`.  Prove

  paperInteraction_sq : paperInteraction M I * paperInteraction M I
    = (((2 : Complex)^q)⁻¹) • 1

in a formulation that does not use negative `Nat` exponents.  A robust target
is `(2^q : Real) • (A*A) = 1`, followed by division using positivity.

  noncomputable def paperHamiltonian
      (M : MajoranaSystem N (Matrix d d Complex))
      (omega : SpinConfig N q) : Matrix d d Complex :=
    sum I, (sigma N q * rademacher omega I : Complex) •
      paperInteraction M I

  noncomputable def normalizedTrace (A : Matrix d d Complex) : Complex :=
    (Fintype.card d : Complex)⁻¹ * Matrix.trace A

  noncomputable def paperPartition
      (beta : Real) (H : Matrix d d Complex) : Real :=
    (normalizedTrace (NormedSpace.exp ((beta : Complex) • H))).re

  noncomputable def annealedPartition (...) : Real :=
    (Fintype.card (SpinConfig N q) : Real)⁻¹ *
      sum omega, paperPartition beta (paperHamiltonian M omega)

Use a finite average over `SpinConfig`, not an abstract probability space, for
the core combinatorial proof.  Later prove that it equals a Bochner integral
under the uniform probability measure.  This removes measurability and
integrability bureaucracy from every finite-volume identity.

Required elementary lemmas:

  card_spinConfig : Fintype.card (SpinConfig N q)
    = 2^(Nat.choose N q)
  sigmaSq_nonneg, sigma_nonneg, sq_sigma
  paperHamiltonian_star_self
  normalizedTrace_one
  paperPartition_pos (hH : star H = H) : 0 < paperPartition beta H
  annealedPartition_pos
  annealedPartition_zero : annealedPartition ... 0 = 1
  annealedPartition_neg : annealedPartition ... (-beta)
    = annealedPartition ... beta

Define

  annealedPressure N q beta := Real.log (annealedPartition ... beta) / N
  annealedFreeEnergy N q beta := Real.log (annealedPartition ... beta) / beta.

The positivity lemmas are essential: every later use of `Real.log_mul`,
`Real.log_div`, or monotonicity of `Real.log` needs explicit nonzero/positive
hypotheses.  Do not postpone them until the final file.

3. FINITE CLIFFORD WORDS AND THE EXACT MOMENT EXPANSION
------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Word sign

Let a word be a list of q-edges.  Define its edge multiplicity and the predicate
that every edge occurs an even number of times:

  abbrev EdgeWord (N q : Nat) := List (Edge N q)
  def edgeMult (w : EdgeWord N q) (I : Edge N q) : Nat := w.count I
  def EvenEdgeWord (w : EdgeWord N q) : Prop :=
    forall I, Even (edgeMult w I)

For even `q`, first prove the interaction commutation rule corresponding to TeX
line 586:

  paperInteraction_commute
    (I J : Edge N q) :
    A_I * A_J = (-1 : Complex)^((I.val ∩ J.val).card) • (A_J * A_I).

Define `wordSign : EdgeWord N q -> {x : Int // x = 1 ∨ x = -1}` on every word by a
canonical sorting/parity algorithm; this is computable and testable.  Prove only
for the subtype of even words that it equals the unique scalar obtained after
Clifford reduction.  Do not use that latter characterization as the definition
on arbitrary words.

Core reduction theorem (TeX lines 587--594):

  product_paperInteraction_of_even
      (hw : EvenEdgeWord w) :
    ∃ r : Nat, w.length = 2*r ∧
      ((w.map (paperInteraction M)).prod) =
        (((wordSign w).1 : Complex) * (((2 : Complex)^q)⁻¹)^r) • 1.

This existential witness avoids ambiguous natural division: extract `r` from
the even length before stating the exponent.  Prove `wordSign` is independent
of the chosen reduction (and note that the length equation makes `r` unique).

3.2 Multi-hypergraph word sum

For a fixed multiplicity function `H`, define the finite set of words realizing
it and the signed word sum (TeX lines 598--605):

  def realizes (H : MultiHypergraph N q) (w : EdgeWord N q) : Prop := ...
  def words (H : MultiHypergraph N q) : Finset (EdgeWord N q) := ...
  def bMulti (H : MultiHypergraph N q) : Int :=
    sum w in words H, wordSign w

It is easier to define `words H` as the permutations of a canonical multiset
than as all lists of a bounded length.  Prove

  card_words = totalMultiplicity H ! / product_I (H.mult I)!
  abs_bMulti_le_card_words
  abs_bMulti_le_factorial : |bMulti H| <= totalMultiplicity H !.

For the connected-component decomposition `H_1,...,H_R`, formalize TeX
equation `eq:b-factorize`:

  bMulti_components :
    bMulti H = multinomial (map totalMultiplicity components) *
      product C in components H, bMulti C.

The proof needs the lemma that interactions on disjoint vertex supports commute.
Make relabelling invariance explicit so a component supported on an arbitrary
subset of `Fin N` can be transported to `Fin v` without changing `bMulti`.

3.3 Rademacher moments and interchange with the exponential series

Prove the finite-product selection rule

  average_rademacher_monomial :
    average omega (product I, rademacher omega I ^ m I)
      = if (forall I, Even (m I)) then 1 else 0.

Expand `Matrix.exp`, commute the finite average with the absolutely convergent
series, and commute normalized trace with the series.  The key intermediate
theorems should be stated independently of hypergraphs:

  normalizedTrace_exp_eq_tsum
  average_tsum_of_uniform_summable
  expected_trace_power_even_word.

Then prove the exact identity at TeX lines 613--645:

  annealedPartition_eq_evenMulti_sum :
    annealedPartition N q beta
      = sum' H : EvenMultiHypergraph N q,
          (beta * paperInteractionScale q * sigma N q)^(totalMultiplicity H) /
            (H.totalMultiplicity.factorial : Real) * (bMulti H : Real).

Again, write powers with nonnegative natural exponents and ordinary positive
denominators; do not literally encode `2^(-q/2)` as a natural power.  The
infinite sum is over total degree, and for each degree only finitely many
multiplicity functions occur.  A safer implementation is

  sum' m : Nat, sum H in evenMultiOfDegree N q m, ...

with a prior absolute-summability bound inherited from the matrix exponential.

Acceptance test: for `beta=0`, only the empty hypergraph remains and the result
simplifies to one.

4. HYPERGRAPHS, CONNECTED COMPONENTS, AND EXCESS
-------------------------------------------------

4.1 Data structures

A simple q-hypergraph on `Fin v` is a wrapped `Finset (Edge v q)` so the later
`.edges` and `.edgeCount` API is uniform:

  structure SimpleHypergraph (v q : Nat) where
    edges : Finset (Edge v q)

  def SimpleHypergraph.edgeCount (T : SimpleHypergraph v q) : Nat :=
    T.edges.card

A multi-hypergraph with arbitrary multiplicities can be represented by a
function `Edge v q -> Nat`; the edge type is finite, so its support is
automatically finite.  A useful structure is:

  structure MultiHypergraph (v q : Nat) where
    mult : Edge v q -> Nat

  def MultiHypergraph.totalMultiplicity (H) : Nat := sum I, H.mult I
  def MultiHypergraph.simpleSupport (H) : Finset (Edge v q) :=
    univ.filter (fun I => H.mult I != 0)
  def MultiHypergraph.even (H) : Prop := forall I, Even (H.mult I)

  abbrev EvenMultiHypergraph (v q : Nat) :=
    {H : MultiHypergraph v q // H.even}

Do not use `Finsupp` unless it materially shortens proofs: on a finite edge type,
ordinary functions give simpler extensionality and sums.

4.2 Connectivity

Define incidence connectivity on vertices: two vertices are adjacent when some
edge in `simpleSupport` contains both; connectedness is reachability in the
reflexive-transitive closure of this relation.  The convention must match TeX
lines 357--370:

  * a one-vertex graph with no edge is connected;
  * for `v>1`, every vertex must lie in the connected support;
  * multi-hypergraph connectivity is connectivity of `simpleSupport`.

Required lemmas:

  connected_iff_auxGraph_connected
  connected_singleton_empty
  connected_supports_all_vertices
  connected_relabel_iff
  exists_unique_connectedComponent_partition
  totalMultiplicity_eq_sum_components.

It may be productive to define an auxiliary `SimpleGraph (Fin v)` whose edges
are the two-sections of the hyperedges and reuse Mathlib's graph connectivity.
Keep the hyperedges themselves as the primary data, because intersection parity
and q-uniformity are lost in the auxiliary graph.

4.3 Hypertrees and the excess type

For a connected simple q-hypergraph `T`, define

  def IsHypertree (T) : Prop :=
    Connected T ∧ (q-1) * T.edgeCount = v-1.

  abbrev SimpleHypertree (v q : Nat) :=
    {T : SimpleHypergraph v q // IsHypertree T}.

Add forwarding lemmas for `.edges` and `.edgeCount` on the subtype, or write
`T.val.edges` consistently in executable code.

This multiplication form avoids truncated natural division.  Prove:

  hypertree_edgeCount (hT) : T.edgeCount = (v-1)/(q-1)
  hypertree_pair_inter_card_le_one
  hypertree_exists_only_if_dvd : (q-1) divides (v-1).

For an even connected multi-hypergraph, encode excess by an equation, not by a
division:

  def HasExcess (H : MultiHypergraph v q) (Delta : Nat) : Prop :=
    H.Connected ∧ H.even ∧
      (q-1) * H.totalMultiplicity = 2*(v-1+Delta)

  abbrev ConnectedEvenOfExcess v q Delta :=
    {H : MultiHypergraph v q // HasExcess H Delta}

The crucial minimality theorem is

  excess_zero_iff_double_hypertree :
    HasExcess H 0 ↔ ∃ T, IsHypertree T ∧
      ∀ I, H.mult I = if I ∈ T.edges then 2 else 0.

This is the rigorous version of `H_(v,0)=T_(v,2)` used throughout the TeX proof.
Its proof requires a lower bound on the number of distinct hyperedges in a
connected q-uniform hypergraph:

  v - 1 <= (q-1) * card H.simpleSupport.

Prove this by adding connected hyperedges one at a time along a spanning tree of
the incidence graph.

4.4 Finiteness and relabelling

Although all multiplicity functions form an infinite type, fixing total
multiplicity makes a finite type when `q` comes from `HTParams`, hence `2<=q`.
Supply instances parameterized by that data, or preferably explicit finsets,
for `ConnectedEvenOfExcess v q Delta`; do not install an invalid global
`Fintype` instance at `q=0` or `q=1`.  Every multiplicity is bounded by
`2*(v-1+Delta)`, so embed it into a finite product of `Fin (bound+1)`.

For an arbitrary component vertex set `C : Finset (Fin N)`, use the increasing
enumeration equivalence `Fin C.card ≃ ↥C` to relabel it to `Fin C.card`.
Prove all weights (`totalMultiplicity`, `bMulti`, connectedness, excess) are
invariant.  Isolate this transport in one module; otherwise dependent casts will
dominate the exponential-formula proof.

5.1 Component weights

        sum H : ConnectedEvenOfExcess v q Delta,
          (beta * paperInteractionScale q * sigma N q)^H.totalMultiplicity /
            H.totalMultiplicity.factorial * bMulti H

Set it to zero when the excess equation has no solutions.  Prove that the
definition is invariant under isomorphic vertex labels.

The singleton coefficient `componentMu N q 1 0 beta = 1` represents unused
vertices, not a physical interaction component.  Record this explicitly; it is
the source of the linear `z` term in `Phi`.

5.2 Formal power series before analytic functions

Use `PowerSeries Real` for the ordered/positive coefficient API, and only later
map coefficients to `Complex`.  Formality in the vertex variable `z` alone is
not enough: for fixed `v`, excess `Delta` and hence edge multiplicity are still
unbounded.  Avoid an illicit infinite rearrangement by first introducing a
second formal marker `u` for total edge multiplicity.  Define finite raw weights
`rawComponentWeight N q v m` by summing graphs of fixed `v` and fixed `m`, and
construct a bivariate series, for example `PowerSeries (PowerSeries Real)`,

  C_N(z,u) = sum_(v,m) rawComponentWeight N q v m * z^v*u^m,
  MSeries2_N = exp(N*C_N).

Every bidegree is a finite sum.  Prove the labelled exponential formula first
coefficientwise in `(v,m)`.  Next prove absolute summability in `m` at the
paper's fixed beta by a crude matrix-exponential majorant, and only then
specialize `u` and rearrange the excess sum.  This yields a univariate real
series `MSeries_N` with component coefficient
`sum' Delta, componentMu N q v Delta beta`.

The reusable specialized formula is:

  coeff_exp_componentSeries :
    coeff N MSeries_N
      = sum over arrays k_(v,Delta) with sum v*k_(v,Delta)=N of
          product_(v,Delta) (N*componentMu N q v Delta)^k / k!.

Combine it with the count of labelled set partitions

  N! / product_(v,Delta) ((v!)^k * k!)

and the component factorization of `bMulti` to obtain the exact proposition at
TeX lines 673--730:

  annealedPartition_eq_factorial_mul_coeff :
    annealedPartition N q beta
      = (N.factorial : Real) / (N : Real)^N *
          coeff N MSeries_N.

Name the real last coefficient `Mcoeff N q beta`; do not call it merely `M_n`, since
the generating function itself depends on `N`.  Prove:

  Mcoeff_pos : 0 < Mcoeff N q beta

from the exact identity and `annealedPartition_pos`, rather than trying to infer
positivity from its signed hypergraph expansion.

When moving to complex analysis, prove that the complex Taylor coefficient is
exactly `(Mcoeff N q beta : Complex)`; do not redefine `Mcoeff` in an unordered
complex coefficient ring.

5.3 Split trees from positive excess

Temporarily name the zero-excess component coefficient

  treeComponentMu N q beta v := componentMu N q v 0 beta.

Section 7 defines `treeMu q beta v` by the chord sum and proves
`treeComponentMu N q beta v = treeMu q beta v`, establishing independence from
`N`.  After that theorem is available, define

  phiC(q,beta,z) = sum' v, treeMu q beta v * z^v,
  B_N(q,beta,z) = N * sum_(1<=v<=N, Delta>=1)
                        componentMu N q v Delta beta * z^v,
  M0_N(z) = exp(N * phiC(z)),
  DeltaM_N(z) = exp(B_N(z)),
  M_N(z) = exp(N * phiC(z) + B_N(z)).

At the formal-series level prove

  MSeries_N = exp(N*treeSeries) * exp(nonTreeSeries_N).

Only after tree and non-tree coefficient bounds are available should these be
promoted to analytic complex functions on `norm z < 2` and continuous functions
on `norm z <= 2`.

Prove an explicit bridge between the formal and analytic objects:

  analytic_taylorSeries_eq_MSeries
  analytic_coeff_eq_powerSeries_coeff
  Mcoeff_eq_circleCoeff.

Without these lemmas, the labelled formal-series coefficient and the contour
coefficient used by the saddle theorem are merely similarly named objects.

6. CHORD SPACE, CROSSING SIGN, AND TREE WORD IDENTITY
-----------------------------------------------------

6.1 Chord probability space

Represent the closed triangle, not all of `[0,1]^2`, because the law is already
ordered:

  def Chord := {x : Real × Real // 0 ≤ x.1 ∧ x.1 ≤ x.2 ∧ x.2 ≤ 1}

Alternatively use the measurable map

  orderPair(u,v) = (min u v, max u v)

and define `chordMeasure` as the pushforward of the product of two normalized
Lebesgue restrictions to `[0,1]`.  Prove once:

  chordMeasure_isProbabilityMeasure
  measurable_orderPair
  ae_chord_endpoints_distinct
  ae_pairwise_distinct_all_endpoints :
    for every finite index type, all endpoints in an independent chord tuple
    are pairwise distinct almost everywhere.

Define `crosses x y` by the two strict interlacing alternatives from TeX lines
424--433 and

  def K (x y : Chord) : Real := if crosses x y then -1 else 1.

Required API:

  measurable_K
  K_comm
  K_self
  abs_K : |K x y| = 1
  K_mul_K : K x y * K x y = 1
  integrable_finite_K_product.

The map `K` is discontinuous on equality boundaries.  Never assert it is
globally continuous.  For continuity of parameterized integrals later, prove
that those boundaries have measure zero and use dominated convergence.

6.2 Crossing weight of a hypergraph

For a simple hypergraph `G` and chord assignment `X : G.edges -> Chord`, define

  crossingWeight G X :=
    product over unordered distinct edge pairs {e,f} with Odd(card(e inter f)),
      K (X e) (X f).

Use unordered pairs (`Sym2` or a filtered product with a fixed linear order) so
each pair appears exactly once.  Prove the value is in `{1,-1}` and has absolute
value one.  Then define

  bSimple G := integral X, crossingWeight G X
    d(Measure.pi (fun _ : ↥G.edges => chordMeasure)).

For rooted `G,r` and fixed virtual chord `x`, include
`product_(e contains r) K x (X e)` and define `bRooted G r x`.  Establish
measurability, integrability, real-valuedness, and

  abs_bSimple_le_one
  abs_bRooted_le_one.

6.3 Exact permutation/chord identity

For a simple hypertree `T`, let `double T` be the multi-hypergraph with edge
multiplicity two.  Prove TeX Lemma `lemma:b_v-vs-b(H)` in the unambiguous form

  (bMulti (double T) : Real) =
    (((2*T.edgeCount).factorial : Real) /
      (2 : Real)^(T.edgeCount)) * bSimple T.

Thus the integer/real coercion and all factorial/power casts are explicit.

Recommended proof:

  1. A word realizing `double T` is a permutation of two labelled occurrences
     of each simple edge, modulo swapping the identical occurrences.
  2. Pair the two positions belonging to each edge.  Two pairs cross exactly
     when commuting the two equal interaction operators together swaps the
     corresponding edge operators an odd number of times.
  3. By `paperInteraction_commute`, that swap contributes
     `(-1)^card(e inter f)`; for a hypertree the intersection cardinal is zero
     or one.
  4. The relative order of `2s` i.i.d. continuous endpoints is a uniform
     permutation almost surely.  Therefore the uniform word average equals
     the continuous chord expectation.
  5. There are `(2s)!/2^s` distinct words.

Do not formalize the paper's interval-jitter construction literally.  The only
needed fact is uniformity of ranks of i.i.d. atomless variables, and crossing
depends solely on their relative order.  Package this as a reusable lemma
`iid_uniform_rank_equiv_uniform_perm` (or prove the finite integral by partitioning
the cube into the `(2s)!` strict-order simplices).

Small regression tests should compute the identity for the singleton tree, a
single q-edge, and two hyperedges intersecting in one vertex.

8. THE CHORD-CAVITY GENERATOR AND `Phi = mathcalF`
---------------------------------------------------

8.1 Analyticity convention

The paper calls functions analytic on a closed disk.  In Lean, separate
analyticity on an open ball from continuity/uniform convergence on its closure.
Use a slightly larger open disk when bounding a closed contour.

8.2 CCG operator

On `D_e = closedBall (0:Complex) (exp(-2))`, define the operator from TeX
`eq:H-def` (lines 449--460).  For `d>=1`, represent chord tuples by
`Fin d -> Chord` and integrate against the finite product measure.

Define named terms

  ccgTerm q G z x d := 1/d! * integral y,
    product i, (K x (y i) * (G z (y i))^(q-1)/(q-1)!) *
    product i<j, K (y i) (y j)

  ccgOp q G z x := z + z * sum' d : {d : Nat // 1 ≤ d}, ccgTerm q G z x d.

Prove absolute and uniform convergence using `|K|=1`, probability mass one,
`norm G<=1`, and comparison with `sum d>=1 1/d! <= exp 1`.

The natural complete space is a closed unit ball in a sup-norm function space.
Two viable implementations are: (A) bounded continuous functions on
`D_e x Chord`, with dominated convergence proving that `ccgOp` preserves
continuity despite discontinuities of `K`; or (B) bounded measurable functions
for the contraction, followed by construction of the continuous/analytic
representative from the rooted-tree series.  Route B is often easier in Lean.

Prove the exact estimates from TeX lines 1148--1268:

  ccgOp_norm_le_one : norm G <= 1 -> norm (ccgOp q G) <= 1
  ccgOp_lipschitz :
    dist (ccgOp q G1) (ccgOp q G2) <= exp(-1) * dist G1 G2
  ccgOp_contracting : ContractingWith (Real.toNNReal (exp(-1))) (ccgOp q)

Use the telescoping power estimate
`norm(a^m-b^m) <= m*norm(a-b)` on the unit ball.  The factor
`(q-1)/(q-1)! <= 1` makes the contraction uniform over `q>=2`.

Apply `ContractingWith.exists_fixedPoint'`/`fixedPoint` from
`Mathlib.Topology.MetricSpace.Contracting` to obtain
`exists_unique_ccg_fixedPoint`, `ccg_fixedPoint`, and `norm_ccg_le_one`.
The canonical definition should take `z : D_e`.  If convenience requires a
total `ccg q : Complex -> Chord -> Complex`, define an explicit extension and
state every fixed-point/tree-series theorem with `norm z<=exp(-2)`.  Do not use
the extension outside that disk as if the paper had defined it there.

8.3 Rooted hypertree expansion

Define

  rootedTreeSeries q z x := sum' v, z^v/v! *
    sum T : SimpleHypertree v q, sum r : Fin v, bRooted T r x.

Prove an exponential coefficient bound before rearranging this sum.  Formalize
the root decomposition at TeX lines 1277--1312: choose the `d` root-incident
edges; each leaves `q-1` rooted branches; distinct branches are disjoint;
root-edge interactions produce `product_(i<j) K(y_i,y_j)`; unordered root

Construct an actual finite equivalence `rootedHypertreeDecompositionEquiv`, not
only a cardinality equation.  With finite-product Fubini prove

  rootedTreeSeries_isFixedPoint :
    ccgOp q (rootedTreeSeries q) = rootedTreeSeries q
  ccg_eq_rootedTreeSeries : ccg q = rootedTreeSeries q.

This proves analytic dependence on `z` by normal convergence and the paper's

8.4 R, E, and corrected unrooting

Define `Rfun` and `Efun` exactly as TeX `eq:Rdef` and `eq:Edef` (lines 505--520):

  Rfun q z = z + z * sum' d>=1, ...

Prove the normally convergent expansions

  Rfun_eq_vertexPointed :
    Rfun q z = sum' v, z^v/v! *
      sum T : SimpleHypertree v q, v * bSimple T

  Efun_eq_edgePointed :

The first points a vertex.  The second points a hyperedge and decomposes it into

Add the regression theorem

8.5 Closed formula and beta zero

9. TREE BOUNDS, ANALYTICITY, AND THE STATIONARY POINT
-----------------------------------------------------

9.1 Replace asymptotic notation by an explicit bound

TeX lines 799--830 use `(1+o(1))` inside a bound that must hold uniformly in
`v`.  Lean needs constants and quantifiers.  Prove either the exact enumeration

  card(SimpleHypertree v q)
    = (v-1)! * v^(s-1) / (s! * ((q-1)!)^s),
    where v-1=(q-1)*s,

or the sufficient consequence

The exact formula can be proved by a generalized Pruefer-code equivalence and
is useful independently; the main theorem only needs the exponential bound.
With `abs_bSimple_le_one`, deduce

All constants depend only on fixed `q`, never on `v`, `N`, or beta in the chosen
high-temperature interval.

9.2 Normal convergence and differentiated series

9.3 Existence and uniqueness of tStar

Fix `cStar` with `0<cStar<1/3` and small enough for the later saddle constants.

11. REUSABLE SADDLE-POINT COEFFICIENT THEOREM
----------------------------------------------

11.1 Isolate the analytic theorem

The saddle proof is independent of SYK once the tree and non-tree bounds are
known.  In `SaddlePoint.lean`, prove the primary theorem along an arbitrary
cofinal size sequence `sizes : Nat -> Nat` with `Tendsto sizes atTop atTop`, for
a fixed analytic `Phi : Complex -> Complex` and
`B : Nat -> Complex -> Complex`.  At index `k`, put `N=sizes k` and

  G_N(z) = Complex.exp (N*Phi(z) + B N z).

Its hypotheses should include:

  * `Phi` and every `B N` are analytic on an open neighborhood of the closed
    disk of radius two;
  * both have real Taylor coefficients, hence take real values on the real axis;
  * `|Phi(z)-z|`, `|Phi'(z)-1|`, and `|z|^2|Phi''(z)|` have the small bounds from
    Section 9;
  * `sup |B N|<=1` and `sup |(B N)'|<=1` uniformly in `N`;
  * `t in (1/2,3/2)` and `t*Phi'(t)=1`;
  * a real sequence `a : Nat -> Real` whose coercion `(a k : Complex)` is the
    degree-`sizes k` Taylor coefficient of `G_(sizes k)`, with `0<a k`.

Write `PhiR t := (Phi (t : Complex)).re`.  The real-coefficient hypothesis also
proves `Phi (t : Complex) = PhiR t`, after coercing the right side to complex.

Conclusion:

  saddlePoint_log_coeff_tendsto :
    Tendsto (fun k => Real.log (a k) / (sizes k : Real)) atTop
      (nhds (PhiR t - Real.log t)).

Assume `0<sizes k` eventually.  An identity-size corollary can use `sizes k=k+1`,
but the SYK application uses the physical even sequence, so coefficient
positivity transfers directly from its finite-volume partition identity.  This
avoids requiring a separate odd-N Majorana representation.

11.2 Cauchy/Fourier coefficient identity

Set

  Psi(theta) = Phi(t * Complex.exp (Complex.I*theta)) - Complex.I*theta.

From the Taylor coefficient definition and Cauchy's integral formula prove

  (a k : Complex) = t^(-N)/(2*pi) * integral theta in [-pi,pi],
    exp (N*Psi(theta) + B N (t*exp(i*theta))),

where throughout Sections 11.2--11.5 `N` abbreviates `sizes k`.

Prefer a named lemma converting `Complex.circleIntegral` to the interval
parameterization, so later estimates use `intervalIntegral`.  Prove the integral
is real by conjugation symmetry and equals its real part.  Keep the distinction
between `B N z` and `re(B N z)` explicit; TeX line 1026 informally drops a real
part inside a real exponential bound.

11.3 Phase estimates

Differentiate `Psi` and use stationarity:

  Psi'(0)=0,
  Psi''(theta) = -t*e^(i theta)*Phi'(t*e^(i theta))
                 -(t*e^(i theta))^2*Phi''(t*e^(i theta)).

Choose the smallness constant once and prove:

  phase_local : |theta|<=1 ->
    re(Psi theta) <= PhiR t - theta^2/8

  phase_outer : 1 ≤ |theta| ∧ |theta| ≤ pi ->
    re(Psi theta) <= PhiR t - delta,

for a fixed `delta>0` (the paper uses `1/5`).  The outer bound follows from
`Phi(z)` being uniformly close to `z` and
`1-cos(theta) >= 1-cos(1)` on the stated compact region.

For the lower bound, obtain a uniform third-derivative constant on
`|theta|<=eta`.  It follows from analyticity on radius two, `t<=3/2`, and a
Cauchy estimate with positive margin.  Prove

  PhiR(t)-theta^2 <= re(Psi theta) <= PhiR(t)-theta^2/8
  |im(Psi theta-Phi(t))| <= C*|theta|^3.

11.4 Upper coefficient bound

Split the integral into `|theta|<=1` and its complement.  From `|B_N|<=1`,
`phase_local`, and `phase_outer`, derive

  a_N <= t^(-N)*exp(N*PhiR(t))*exp(1)/(2*pi) *
    (sqrt(8*pi/N) + 2*pi*exp(-delta*N)).

Turn this explicit bound into

  limsup (Real.log(a_N)/N) <= PhiR(t)-log(t).

Prove separate helper limits for `log(C/sqrt N)/N` and exponentially small
summands.  Positivity of `a_N` is needed before applying `Real.log_le_iff`.

11.5 Lower coefficient bound and cancellation control

Fix universal `L>=1` later and choose `eta in (0,1]`.  For large `N`, split
`[-pi,pi]` into

  central: |theta| <= L/sqrt N,
  middle:  L/sqrt N <= |theta| <= 1,
  outer:   1 <= |theta|.

Since `B_N` is real on the real axis and `|(B_N)'|<=1`, control the phase drift
along the circle by

  |im(B_N(t*e^(i theta))-B_N(t))| <= C_B*|theta|.

For fixed `L`, the total imaginary phase on the central interval is bounded by

  N*C*|theta|^3 + C_B*|theta| <= (C*L^3+C_B*L)/sqrt N,

which is eventually at most `pi/3`.  Thus the real part of the central integral
is at least one half its modulus lower bound.  Obtain

  central_N >= exp(N*PhiR(t))*exp(-2)/sqrt N *
    integral u in [-L,L], exp(-u^2).

Choose `L` so the Gaussian middle tail is at most one third of this bound, and
then choose `N` so the outer exponential tail is also at most one third.  The
full integral is at least one third of `central_N`.  This proves

  liminf (Real.log(a_N)/N) >= PhiR(t)-log(t).

Formalize symmetric regions with `abs theta`; TeX item 3 at line 1124 writes
`theta>=1` although the integral uses `|theta|>=1`.

11.6 Finish by limsup/liminf or epsilon squeeze

Either combine the two bounds using `Filter.limsup`/`liminf`, or formulate
eventual exponential bounds: for every `epsilon>0`, eventually

  exp(N*(PhiR(t)-log(t)-epsilon)) <= a_N
  a_N <= exp(N*(PhiR(t)-log(t)+epsilon)).

The latter interface is usually easier to reuse and makes the final log limit a
short squeeze proof.  Keep all constants (`cStar`, `delta`, `eta`, `L`, third-
derivative bound) fields or consequences of a single `SaddleCertificate`.

12. ASSEMBLY OF THE ANNEALED PRESSURE AND FREE-ENERGY THEOREMS
--------------------------------------------------------------

12.1 Coefficient asymptotics

Instantiate `saddlePoint_log_coeff_tendsto` with

  sizes k = admissibleSize q k,
  Phi = phiC q beta,
  B N = B_N q beta,
  t = tStar q beta,
  a k = Mcoeff (admissibleSize q k) q beta.

Discharge its assumptions using the high-temperature certificate, tree bounds,
non-tree bounds, stationary-point API, real-coefficient lemmas, and
`Mcoeff_pos`.  The result corresponding to TeX Proposition `prop:M_n` is

  tendsto_log_Mcoeff_div_even :
    Tendsto (fun k =>
      Real.log (Mcoeff (admissibleSize q k) q beta) /
        (admissibleSize q k : Real)) atTop
      (nhds (phiR q beta (tStar q beta) - Real.log (tStar q beta))).

Be careful that `Mcoeff N` is the degree-N coefficient of a generating function
that also depends on `N`.

12.2 The missing `-1`: factorial asymptotics

From the exact finite-volume identity,

  log(annealedPartition N q beta)/N
    = log(N!)/N - log N + log(Mcoeff N q beta)/N.

The first two terms converge to `-1`.  Prove and keep as a reusable lemma

  tendsto_log_factorial_div_sub_log :
    Tendsto
      (fun N => Real.log (N.factorial : Real)/N - Real.log N)
      atTop (nhds (-1)).

Derive it from `Mathlib.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.Stirling`, in particular
`Stirling.log_stirlingSeq_formula` or `factorial_isEquivalent_stirling`.
Handle `N=0` separately or state it along `N+1` and transfer.  This step is what
produces the `-1` in TeX equation `eq:main-result`; it should not be hidden in a
large `simpa`.

12.3 Assemble along physical even sizes

Prove

  admissibleSize_even
  q_le_admissibleSize
  tendsto_admissibleSize_atTop.

Compose the Stirling limit with `admissibleSize q k=q+2*k` and combine it with
`tendsto_log_Mcoeff_div_even` and the exact finite-volume identity.  This yields

  annealedPressure_tendsto_even_sizes :
    Tendsto
      (fun k => annealedPressure (admissibleSize q k) q beta)
      atTop
      (nhds (phiR q beta (tStar q beta)-1-Real.log(tStar q beta))).

Thus neither positivity nor a Hilbert space is needed at odd `N`.  Conditions
such as `q<=N` are automatic along this sequence.

12.4 Convert Phi to the paper formula

Use `phiC_eq_closedFormulaC` and the real-restriction lemma at `tStar` to rewrite
the pressure limit as

  closedFormulaR q beta (tStar q beta) - 1 - log(tStar q beta).

For `beta!=0`, use

  annealedFreeEnergy N q beta / N
    = annealedPressure N q beta / beta

and continuity of multiplication/division by a fixed nonzero beta to obtain the
requested formula.  Supply both positive-temperature and signed-beta corollaries:

  annealed_freeEnergy_density_limit_of_pos
    (hbeta : 0<beta) (hsmall : beta<=betaStar q)

  annealed_freeEnergy_density_limit
    (hbeta : beta!=0) (hsmall : |beta|<=betaStar q).

Use the exact symmetry lemmas

  annealedPartition_neg
  phiC_neg_beta
  phiR_neg_beta
  tStar_neg_beta
  closedFormulaR_neg_beta

to derive the negative-beta case if the matrix positivity API is initially
developed only for positive beta.

12.5 Beta zero statement

Prove separately

  annealedPartition_zero : annealedPartition N q 0=1
  annealedPressure_zero : annealedPressure N q 0=0
  annealed_pressure_limit_zero.

If a total extended free-energy density is desired, define

  extendedAnnealedFEDensity N q beta :=
    if beta=0 then 0 else annealedFreeEnergy N q beta/N

and prove its thermodynamic formula with the same piecewise convention.  Do not
call `annealedFreeEnergy ... 0` the paper's free energy without this definition.

12.6 Final `main_result.lean` skeleton

The assembly file should be short and contain no combinatorial or analytic
proofs.  Its shape should be approximately:

  import SYK.Annealed_FE.Asymptotics
  import SYK.Annealed_FE.CCG
  import SYK.Annealed_FE.ConcreteMajorana

  noncomputable section
  open scoped BigOperators
  open Filter Topology

  namespace SYK.AnnealedFE

  theorem annealed_pressure_limit
      (q : Nat) (hq2 : 2<=q) (hqeven : Even q)
      (beta : Real) (hsmall : |beta|<=betaStar q) :
      Tendsto
        (fun k => annealedPressure (admissibleSize q k) q beta)
        atTop
        (nhds (closedFormulaR q beta (tStar q beta) - 1 -
          Real.log (tStar q beta))) := by
    ...

  theorem annealed_freeEnergy_density_limit
      (q : Nat) (hq2 : 2<=q) (hqeven : Even q)
      (beta : Real) (hbeta : beta!=0)
      (hsmall : |beta|<=betaStar q) :
      Tendsto
        (fun k => annealedFreeEnergy (admissibleSize q k) q beta /
          (admissibleSize q k : Real))
        atTop
        (nhds ((closedFormulaR q beta (tStar q beta) - 1 -
          Real.log (tStar q beta))/beta)) := by
    ...

  end SYK.AnnealedFE

The actual theorem may take a paper-model structure or concrete Majorana
instance as an argument.  If so, add a final theorem with no abstract model
argument that instantiates the Jordan--Wigner system.

16. FORMALIZATION RISKS AND DECISIONS THAT MUST NOT BE HIDDEN
-------------------------------------------------------------

1. Beta zero.  The TeX free energy and closed formula divide by zero there.
   Pressure is the primary theorem; free energy requires `beta!=0`.

2. Even system sizes.  The physical Hilbert dimension requires even `N`.
   Use a cofinal even sequence, not natural-number truncation of `N/2`.

3. Majorana normalization.  Repository generators square to one; paper
   generators square to one-half.  Missing the factor `2^(-q/2)` changes rho and
   every tree weight.

4. Exponential sign and trace.  Repository thermodynamics uses `exp(-beta H)`
   and ordinary trace; the paper uses `exp(+beta H)` and normalized trace.
   Prove named bridges and beta symmetry.

5. Rademacher versus Gaussian.  This TeX proof expands Rademacher moments.
   Gaussian Wick pairings are not interchangeable here: higher even Gaussian
   moments change the positive-excess weights.  Universality is external and is
   not a shortcut for the requested theorem.

6. Double-counted singleton.  Correct TeX lines 1348--1360 as in Section 0.4
   and test the linear coefficient.

7. Connected graph count.  Stars-and-bars counts all multiplicity assignments,
   so it is an upper bound, not an equality, for connected hypergraphs.

8. Analyticity on a closed disk.  Translate it into open-disk analyticity plus
   closed-disk continuity and uniform bounds.

9. Discontinuous crossing kernel.  `K` is only measurable globally.  Parameter
   continuity of its integrals needs boundary-null and dominated-convergence
   lemmas.

10. Positivity before logarithms.  Signed hypergraph coefficients do not make
    positivity obvious.  Transfer it from the positive trace exponential via
    the exact finite-volume identity.

11. Name shadowing.  The TeX symbol `M_n` is the nth coefficient of a function
    that itself depends on `n`.  Use `Mcoeff N` and `MSeries N` separately.

12. Natural-number division.  Excess and tree edge counts should be equations
    with multiplication until a divisibility witness has been extracted.

13. Uniformity in series order.  Fixed-order moment convergence cannot prove an
    exponential partition-function limit.  The formal proof must retain the
    exact generating function and uniform coefficient bounds.

14. Lower-bound cancellation.  An upper bound on the modulus of a contour
    integral cannot prove the saddle lower bound.  The central phase, middle
    Gaussian tail, and outer tail must be controlled separately.

15. Paper shorthand around `b(H)`.  Distinguish simple edge count `s`, total
    multi-edge count `2s`, number of words `(2s)!/2^s`, and the factorial in the
    definition of component weights.  Never overload one `m` for all four.

16. Scope.  Positivity of the limiting free energy, the ground-state corollary,
    quenched concentration, and Gaussian universality are not proved in the
    supplied main argument.  Keep them out of the theorem unless their external
    inputs are formalized separately.

17. Source completeness.  The proof block at TeX lines 1137--1142 only says the
    result is completed and defers loose ends; the `-1` from `N!/N^N` must still
    appear explicitly in Lean through Stirling.

18. Project layout.  Do not let a theorem appear proved only because Lean is
    resolving a different copy of `SYK` through an accidental `LEAN_PATH`.
    CI must build from a clean canonical checkout.
